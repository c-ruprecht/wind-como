The Breva model

Two small machine-learning models answer two questions: will there be a Breva (14+ kn on the upper lake for at least 2 hours) and will it be a long one (at least 5 hours)? The 7-day model looks only at the weather forecast. The today model adds what the stations have measured so far.

Breva days and long Breva days per year are counted from March to October. Skill is the AUC on real forecasts 2024–2026 that the model had not seen in training: 0.5 is guessing, 1.0 would be perfect.

How it works

  • Training uses ERA5, the weather as it actually was (a reanalysis, 25 km), for every day since 2014 – about 4,700 days with a known outcome. A trained model only needs the 37 variables, which the ECMWF forecast provides for the next 7 days.
  • Calibration is what makes the percentage honest. For each year since 2024 a model is trained on all other years and then fed the real ECMWF forecasts of that year, 1 to 7 days ahead. From these predictions a curve per day-ahead translates the model score into “on how many such days was there really a Breva”. Forecast errors are therefore already included: the further ahead, the more cautious the chance.
  • Why the model and not a weather model's wind directly? A 25 km weather model does not resolve the lake: it shows 2–3 kn of south wind on a 20 kn Breva day. What it does get right are the drivers – sun, pressure gradient, dry air, large-scale flow – and the model learns how they add up to a Breva.

What is predicted

The truth the model learns from comes from the stations, scaled to the lake with the Sentinel-1 satellite:

  • Breva day: Samolaco or Colico – multiplied by the satellite factor ( and , wind on the lake vs. at the station on Breva evenings) and only counting wind from the Breva direction – shows 14+ kn for at least 2 hours in a row between 11 and 20:00. No wind from another direction in the morning, no north wind.
  • Long Breva: a Breva day with at least 5 hours of 14+ kn.
  • Checked against the satellite: on calm days the lake is smooth at 19:15 (5 % rough), on long Breva days 73 % rough. Short Breva days and near misses (only 1 hour, or 10–14 kn) look the same on the satellite – the line between them is fluid, which is why a 50 % day is often a weak Breva.

What makes a Breva – and what makes it last

For each weather variable: how clearly does it separate calm days from short Breva days, and short from long ones? Right of zero = higher on the Breva (or long) days. Measured with ERA5 on all days March–October 2014–2026.

  • Breva or not: south wind over the lake in the weather model, sun in the valley and in the plain, warm air over a cool lake, dry air, season. Rain, north foehn and wet soil work against it.
  • Long or short: partly the same drivers, only stronger – more south wind, more late-afternoon sun, drier air. But three variables matter only for the duration (marked ◆): a stronger pressure gradient from the plain into the valleys at 15:00 (Bergamo → Sondrio, Milan → Chiavenna) and rising pressure over the previous 24 hours, i.e. a building high.
  • Heat alone does not help: maximum temperatures are as high on short as on long Breva days. Short days are often hot days with a weaker gradient or afternoon showers.

The today model

Every two hours the today model combines the forecast for today with what has been measured so far. Until about 9:00 it mostly relies on the forecast; from 11:00 the stations take over – Vercana above Domaso turns south-west first, from 13:00 the measured wind on the lake decides. Whether a Breva will be long becomes clear in the early afternoon.

Limits

  • About half of all Breva days stay uncertain the day before. Most of them are borderline: a weak Breva came, and whether it reached 14 kn for 2 hours was a matter of one hour. Sun is the biggest unknown – the measured sun on the day separates these days better than any forecast variable.
  • No station on the water. The wind on the lake is estimated from Samolaco and Colico with a factor from the satellite. A station at Domaso, Gravedona or Dervio would sharpen both the target and the today model.
  • One forecast run. The ECMWF ensemble (51 runs) would show how certain a day is; it is now being archived and can be tested after one season.
  • Test period 2024–2026: about 700 days March–October per day-ahead. Differences of ±0.02 AUC are within the noise.
